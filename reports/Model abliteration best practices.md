# Modern Abliteration Trades Brute Force for Optimization

As of October 2026, the best way to abliterate an open-weight LLM is no longer the 2024 recipe of subtracting one hand-picked difference-in-means vector from the weights. The state of the art is **optimization-driven, KL-constrained weight editing**: compute per-layer refusal directions from the first generated token, project them against the "harmless" direction, preserve weight-row norms, apply them to attention output projections (and sparingly to MLP down-projections) across a band of middle layers with layer-tapered strengths, and let a multi-objective optimizer co-minimize a refusal score and the first-token KL divergence from the original model. The dominant tool, **Heretic**, reaches the same refusal suppression as earlier hand-tuned checkpoints at roughly **3-6x lower KL divergence** (Gemma-3-12B: 3/100 refusals at KL 0.16 versus 0.45-1.04 for prior abliterations) ([Heretic README](https://github.com/p-e-w/heretic)), and its 2026 default has shifted further, to a direction-free per-module regression (ARA). This works because safety alignment is shallow and formulaic: it concentrates a near-rank-one "begin refusal" feature at the chat-template tokens, downstream of and separable from the model's harmfulness judgment ([Arditi et al., NeurIPS 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11717v3); [Zhao et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11878); [Labunets 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25390)). But the single-direction picture is incomplete — refusal lives in multi-dimensional cones ([Wollschläger et al., ICML 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17420)) — and the technique fails against models deliberately hardened to spread refusal across many tokens or independent subspaces, where fine-tuning remains the more complete and defense-agnostic attack. The recurring and under-appreciated caveat is evaluation: keyword-based refusal counts, which almost every tool optimizes against, substantially overstate success, and "no refusal" is not "useful output."

## One direction carries refusal, and erasing it leaves the model otherwise intact

The foundational method is **"Refusal in Language Models Is Mediated by a Single Direction"** (Arditi, Obeso, Syed, Paleka, Panickssery, Gurnee, Nanda), published at **NeurIPS 2024** ([arXiv 2406.11717](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11717v3)). Its procedure is austere: run a set of harmful instructions (128 sampled from AdvBench, MaliciousInstruct and TDC2023) and a matched set of harmless ones (128 from Alpaca) through the model, and at each layer and each post-instruction chat-template token position take the **difference in means** between the two activation sets, r = μ_harmful − μ_harmless. From these candidates the authors select one by validation scores — minimize a bypass score subject to an induction score above zero, a harmless-prompt KL below 0.1, and a layer index below 80% of depth (to stay away from token-level features near the unembedding) ([arXiv 2406.11717 App. C.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11717v3)). The selected unit vector is then **ablated** everywhere: every residual-stream activation x has its component along r̂ removed, x′ = x − r̂r̂ᵀx. Crucially, this inference-time intervention is **exactly equivalent to a rank-one weight edit** — for every matrix that writes to the residual stream (token embedding, each attention output projection, each MLP down-projection), set W′ = W − r̂r̂ᵀW — because once every earlier writer is orthogonalized, r̂ᵀx is zero at each write ([arXiv 2406.11717 App. E](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11717v3)). That weight edit is what the community nicknamed "abliteration," and it costs **less than $5 of compute even for a 70B model**, needs no gradients and no harmful completions, only prompts ([arXiv 2406.11717 §7](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11717v3)).

The intervention is strikingly surgical. Across 13 chat models from 1.8B to 72B, benchmark scores stay mostly within noise: Llama-3 70B moves from 79.9 to 79.8 on MMLU and 91.2 to 90.8 on GSM8K after orthogonalization ([arXiv 2406.11717 Table 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11717v3)). Ablation is more surgical than the alternative of simply subtracting r, because projecting out r̂ moves harmful activations toward the harmless manifold without pushing harmless activations off-distribution — on-distribution cross-entropy for Llama-3 8B is 0.213 for ablation versus 0.441 for subtraction (baseline 0.195) ([arXiv 2406.11717 App. I.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11717v3)). The one consistent casualty is **TruthfulQA**, which drops 1.0 to 3.5 points for every model, because its categories (misinformation, conspiracies, stereotypes) "veer close to the territory of refusal" ([arXiv 2406.11717 App. G.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11717v3)).

Why does a *single* direction suffice? The best-supported account synthesizes four findings. First, safety alignment is **shallow**: it adapts the model's distribution primarily over the first few output tokens, which is why suffix, prefilling and fine-tuning attacks all work ([Qi et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.05946)). Second, because refusals open with near-identical formulaic phrases across every harm category, the first-token cross-entropy gradients during safety training concentrate the learned change into a **low-stable-rank subspace** — a near-rank-one "begin refusal" feature; diversifying refusal openings raises the stable rank and measurably weakens single-vector ablation ([Labunets 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25390)). Third, this feature is a **decision signal anchored to the template tokens**, distinct from the model's harmfulness judgment: the refusal direction (read at the post-instruction tokens) and the harmfulness direction (read at the last instruction token) have cosine similarity around **0.1** on Llama-2, and simply deleting the template tokens drops Llama-3-8B's AdvBench refusal from **96.0% to 58.9%** ([Zhao et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11878)). Fourth, the harmful/harmless distinction **pre-exists in the base model** and is merely amplified and routed into refusal during post-training ([arXiv 2406.11717 App. J](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11717v3); [Kissane et al. 2024](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/YWo2cKJgL7Lg8xWjj/base-llms-refuse-too)). Abliteration thus removes the *expression* of refusal while leaving the model's latent recognition of harm — and its knowledge — in place, which is why abliterated models still add caveats and can still describe why a request is objectionable ([arXiv 2406.11717 App. L](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11717v3)).

The caveat the authors themselves raise is that "single direction" is best read as "a dominant, shared direction sufficient for most refusals in most standardly-aligned models," not "refusal is one-dimensional." Peer-reviewed follow-up shows refusal is mediated by **polyhedral cones of up to five dimensions** across all tested Gemma-2, Llama-3 and Qwen2.5 models, that orthogonal directions are not independent under intervention, and that different refusal *categories* use geometrically distinct directions that nonetheless act as "a shared one-dimensional control knob" ([Wollschläger et al., ICML 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17420); [Joad et al., EMNLP 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02132)). This multi-dimensionality is exactly what explains both residual refusals after a single-pass ablation and why "spread the refusal" defenses work.

## The field ranks: optimize and constrain first, add directions only when stuck

The refinements since 2024 sort into a clear hierarchy, which doubles as a recommended escalation ladder. Weighting peer-reviewed evidence over preprints over self-reported tool numbers, the ordering is: **optimization-constrained editing first; multi-direction ablation when single-pass refusals plateau; direction-cleaning to cut collateral damage; gradient-optimized directions for minimal side effects; and plain difference-in-means as the baseline to beat.**

At the top sits **optimization-driven, KL-constrained editing**, the Heretic approach. Rather than pick one direction and apply it uniformly, it computes per-layer directions, lets a float-valued `direction_index` interpolate between adjacent layers' directions, and parameterizes a per-component ablation-weight kernel over layers (separate weights for attention versus MLP) that an Optuna TPE optimizer tunes to co-minimize a keyword refusal rate and first-token KL ([Heretic README](https://github.com/p-e-w/heretic)). The measured payoff is the cleanest quantitative result in the field: on Gemma-3-12B, Heretic matches the 3/100 refusal rate of hand-tuned checkpoints at **KL 0.16 versus 0.45 (huihui-ai) and 1.04 (mlabonne-v2)** ([Heretic README](https://github.com/p-e-w/heretic)). Two design choices drive this: letting the MLP kernel fall to zero (MLP edits "tend to damage model intelligence more than ablating the attention output"), and **norm preservation** — restoring each weight row's original L2 norm after the rank-one edit, implemented as a rank-3 LoRA approximation ([Heretic configuration](https://heretic-project.org/configuration)). Heretic also folds in **projected abliteration** (grimjim, Oct 2025): ablate only the component of r orthogonal to the harmless mean, r_proj = r − (r·μ̂_H)μ̂_H, which shrinks disturbance to harmless activations ([grimjim, Projected Abliteration](https://huggingface.co/blog/grimjim/projected-abliteration)). Both projection and norm preservation (grimjim's "MPOA") are best understood as low-cost hygiene on a rank-one edit; the standalone evidence for MPOA is thin — essentially one UGI/NatInt leaderboard row (Gemma-3-12B: 32.61/21.33 versus 32.08/18.64 for standard abliteration) ([grimjim, Norm-Preserving Biprojected Abliteration](https://huggingface.co/blog/grimjim/norm-preserving-biprojected-abliteration)) — but it composes cleanly with the optimizer and is now a default.

As of late 2026 Heretic's default has shifted again, to **Arbitrary-Rank Ablation (ARA)**, which abandons refusal directions entirely: it captures each module's input/output with hooks and solves an unconstrained per-module regression (a LoRA, default rank 50, via L-BFGS) with three goals — keep harmless outputs unchanged, pull harmful outputs toward their nearest harmless neighbors, and push harmful outputs away from their originals ([Heretic PR #211](https://github.com/p-e-w/heretic/pull/211)). This is a conceptual shift from "remove a direction" to "constrained output steering," and informal numbers suggest it is the only method reliably removing refusal from hard targets like gpt-oss, though it has no peer-reviewed evaluation.

When single-pass ablation plateaus — typically at 10-30% residual refusals on strongly-aligned models — the evidence favors **multi-direction ablation**. The strongest peer-reviewed result is **SOM** (Piras et al., **AAAI 2026**): train a self-organizing map over harmful-prompt representations to yield many candidate directions, then select a subset (k ≈ 2-7) by Bayesian optimization. Ablating them raises HarmBench ASR far above single-direction DIM — **Llama-3-8B 88.05 versus 15.09, Gemma-2-9B 96.27 versus 38.93** — and beats specialized jailbreak algorithms on most models ([Piras et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08379)). Most models saturate by k ≈ 3-5, consistent with the ≤5-D cone geometry, and the method has been ported into Heretic as "SOMA"/"SOMPOA." **Gradient-optimized directions (RDO)** sit slightly below multi-direction on efficacy but above it on side effects: RDO roughly ties DIM on ASR while cutting ablation-induced TruthfulQA error by about **40%** through an explicit KL "retain" loss ([Wollschläger et al., ICML 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.17420v2)). **Direction-cleaning** methods generalize projected abliteration by residualizing the dirty difference vector against a registry of protected concept directions; Surgical Refusal Ablation reports first-token KL of **0.016-0.044 versus 0.097-2.088** for standard ablation at equal or lower refusal across five models, though it is single-author and self-evaluated ([Cristofano 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08489)). A useful negative result from this cluster: direction quality depends heavily on contrast-set design — **topic-matched** harmless prompts can cancel the large topic-distance component and leave a difference vector too small to function, so broad, unmatched harmless contrasts are preferred ([Petrov 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.22061v1)).

The honest summary is that **no peer-reviewed, multi-model head-to-head compares all of these on shared KL-and-benchmark metrics**, and KL conventions differ across sources (first-token versus multi-token, batchmean versus per-prompt), so cross-source numbers are not strictly comparable. What is robust: a single global direction at uniform strength across all layers is no longer best practice; strength should taper across a band of middle layers and be tuned against an explicit KL budget.

## Tooling and architecture decide what actually works

Practice has professionalized around two automated tools. **Heretic** (first public release Nov 2025, dev 2.0 by Oct 2026) is the default for dense, most MoE, and VL models; **Abliterix** is a close competitor adding explicit mechanisms for mixture-of-experts, FP4 and alternative steering methods ([Heretic README](https://github.com/p-e-w/heretic); [Abliterix README](https://github.com/wuwangzhang1216/abliterix)). Both inherit Arditi's defaults almost verbatim — `mlabonne/harmful_behaviors` (repackaged AdvBench) as the harmful set, `mlabonne/harmless_alpaca` as harmless, the chat template applied with a generation prompt, and the residual taken at the last prompt / first generated token. A recurring practical lesson is that the **language of the extraction prompts matters**: the refusal direction co-localizes with language features, so extracting with English prompts can collaterally damage other languages — one Traditional-Chinese VLM lost coherent zh-TW generation until its builder switched to Chinese contrast pairs ([Qwen3-VL-32B-Abliterated card](https://huggingface.co/xCloudinfo/Qwen3-VL-32B-Abliterated-xCloud)).

**Mixture-of-experts** models are where naive scripts silently fail. Refusal is distributed across many routed experts rather than isolated in a router or a few "safety experts," so the robust default is to orthogonalize the `down_proj` of *every* expert in every MoE layer, plus attention output ([Abliterix docs/moe.md](https://github.com/wuwangzhang1216/abliterix/blob/master/docs/moe.md)). The single most important recent MoE finding reframes "which modules" as a *joint-intervention* problem: on a **320B MoE** (GLM-5.3-Flash, 288 experts), editing attention, dense and expert writers alone removed little refusal (0.039, 0.016, 0.148 respectively), but editing all three jointly removed **0.776 — and 74% of that effect appeared only under joint intervention**, while conventional module-name matching captured only 0.066 ([Shi et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09793)). Directional ablation "survives the architecture" up to frontier scale, but module-name matching "can fail silently on MoE models." Router suppression (negatively biasing safety-linked experts' routing logits) is an optional extra that can over-disturb response-length behavior; one independent comparison found a router-targeting run hit a **49.2% empty-response rate** on GSM8K ([Abliterlitics GLM-4.7-Flash](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1sy18lx/snapshots/2026-05-02T03%3A06%3A21.60641Z)).

**Reasoning models** are the hardest case because refusal is jointly encoded in activations *and* in the chain of thought. Activation-only ablation works "less reliably" than on instruction models and inflicts "non-negligible degradation to general capabilities" ([Yamaguchi, Etheridge, Arditi 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.03167)). The cleanest decomposition: on a DeepSeek-R1 distill, steering reversed refusal in ~39% of cases with the CoT fixed, ~70% with the CoT removed, and **~94% when the model regenerated its CoT under steering** — the CoT actively reinforces refusal ([Yang et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26772)). Tools handle this by skipping the closed think block so extraction and evaluation happen at the start of the real answer (Heretic's `chain_of_thought_skips`), but visible compliance overstates success: even at 100% visible ASR, abliterated reasoning models still showed safety deliberation in 39-60% of their CoTs ([Abliterlitics GLM-4.7-Flash](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1sy18lx/snapshots/2026-05-02T03%3A06%3A21.60641Z)). gpt-oss is the standing counterexample to the single-direction thesis: single-direction ablation only reached 58/100 refusals, and a direction taken from CoT tokens flipped essentially none on held-out prompts ([Reblitz-Richardson 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14759)); direction-free ARA or CoT-regeneration approaches are needed there.

**Multimodal** abliteration is almost always text-only — extract and ablate on the language tower, leave the vision encoder untouched, and prefer a single direction "for simplicity and compatibility with the vision tower" ([sci4ai Qwen2.5-VL card](https://huggingface.co/sci4ai/Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct-Ablit)). No documented tool targets refusals conditioned on image inputs. On **quantization**, the universal ordering is to abliterate the full-precision (or dequantized) weights first and quantize to GGUF last; a direction measured on a 4-bit quant transfers to the full BF16 model, and native-FP4 models (gpt-oss MXFP4) must be dequantized to BF16 to edit, then optionally re-packed ([grimjim, Projected Abliteration](https://huggingface.co/blog/grimjim/projected-abliteration); [wangzhang gpt-oss-20b card](https://huggingface.co/wangzhang/gpt-oss-20b-abliterated)). Heretic keeps the edit as a small LoRA adapter riding on a frozen quantized base, so the quantized weights stay intact. No source has measured whether refusals drift back after aggressive post-abliteration quantization — a genuine open question.

## Keyword counts lie; honest evaluation needs judges, KL budgets and off-target checks

The single biggest source of inflated published refusal numbers is the evaluation method, and a writer or practitioner should treat most model-card "3/100" claims as not comparable. Every major tool, and most papers, score refusal with **substring or keyword matching on ~100 prompts**. The one independent cross-check found this gives **72.2% attack-success versus 95.7% from a RoBERTa classifier**, with the gap running from +6.7 points on Llama to +50 points on Qwen2.5 — mostly because compliant-but-caveated answers get miscounted as refusals ([Young 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.13655v1)). Heretic's keyword list includes topical words like "illegal," "harmful," "unethical" and "disclaimer," which means it *overstates* residual refusals and simultaneously pushes the optimizer toward dropping caveats rather than merely complying ([Heretic config.default.toml](https://github.com/p-e-w/heretic/blob/master/config.default.toml)). Short generations compound the problem: on one model, 30-token scoring found ~8/100 refusals, 50-token ~18/100, and 100-token a converged **60/100** — delayed refusals hide behind a compliant opening ([Abliterix docs/evaluation.md](https://github.com/wuwangzhang1216/abliterix/blob/master/docs/evaluation.md)).

The recommended evaluation stack reflects these pitfalls. Use a keyword rate only for the fast inner optimization loop. For reporting, use an **LLM judge** (StrongREJECT rubric, HarmBench classifier, Llama Guard, or a SORRY-Bench judge) on full-length outputs over a held-out set disjoint from the extraction prompts, and separate the CoT from the final answer for reasoning models. Critically, score *quality*, not just non-refusal: StrongREJECT weights willingness by specificity and convincingness precisely because "no refusal" is not "useful answer" ([StrongREJECT 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.10260)). Confirm over-refusal fell too (XSTest, OR-Bench). For capability damage, the cheap proxy is **first-token KL on ~100 harmless prompts** — Arditi's selection filter used KL < 0.1 and Heretic's best Gemma result is KL 0.16 — but it is blind to drift in long generations and to CoT changes ([Heretic kl_divergence.py](https://github.com/p-e-w/heretic/blob/master/src/heretic/scorers/kl_divergence.py)). The empirical damage signature is consistent: knowledge benchmarks (MMLU, ARC, HellaSwag) barely move and are poor detectors; **TruthfulQA reliably drops 1-3.5 points and is the canary**; **GSM8K/multi-step math is the most fragile**, ranging from +1.5 to **−18.8 points** depending on tool and model (−26.5% relative on Yi-1.5-9B under Heretic) ([Young 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.13655v1)); reasoning models lose 5-30% relative on AIME/GPQA/BBEH ([Yamaguchi et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.03167)). An independent weight-forensics benchmark puts the damage tripwire at **KL above ~0.3**, with IFEval the earliest warning signal and the most surgical edits (pure attention-output, ~29 tensors) preserving capability best ([Abliterlitics Gemma-4-E4B](https://huggingface.co/DreamFast/Gemma4-e4b-abliterlitics)).

Two deeper points matter for anyone deploying the result. First, KL and residual refusals correlate at **r = 0.87** — models that resist refusal removal also pay the largest distribution cost, so per-model tuning and matched-refusal comparisons are essential; neither Heretic's README nor the one cross-tool study compared at matched refusal *and* matched compute ([Young 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.13655v1)). Second, abliteration is **"not a scalpel"**: a preregistered study on two MoE models found abliterated versions became measurably more optimistic (+12.2 and +7.4 points) with instruction-following intact, concluding the abliterated model is "a measurably different decision-maker," not the base model minus its refusals ([Fafuła 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17427)). Benchmarks that look fine can hide disposition shifts that surface in agentic use.

## Fine-tuning wins on completeness and against hardened models

Against a standard refusal-trained model, abliteration is the right tool when you have only prompts (no harmful completions), little compute, and want a portable, mostly capability-preserving edit. But it is not uniformly superior to its main alternative. **Fine-tuning attacks** are equally cheap — Qi et al. raised GPT-3.5's harmfulness from 1.8% to 88.8% with **10 examples for under $0.20**, Lermen et al. stripped Llama-2-70B to ~1% refusal for **under $200**, and Shadow Alignment broke eight models with **100 examples and one GPU-hour** ([Qi et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.03693); [Lermen et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20624); [Shadow Alignment 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02949)). The real differences are: abliteration needs only prompts while fine-tuning needs completions or a reward signal; fine-tuning reaches ~0-1% refusal while single-direction abliteration often leaves **3-30%**; and fine-tuning transfers better to downstream task *utility*. The one controlled head-to-head (small Qwen2.5 models, authorized-security tasks) favored LoRA decisively — **0.87 security score versus ~0.50 for single-vector projection** — and found single-vector projection *increased* out-of-scope unsafe output (spillover 0.47 versus 0.10), calling projection "surface removal" ([Gervais & Gervais 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.17413v1)). The pragmatic community pipeline is a hybrid: KL-constrained abliteration, an optional second pass on the residual direction, and a short **DPO heal** to recover capability — though healing is imperfect (Labonne's NeuralDaredevil recovered its Nous-suite average from 55.06 to 55.87 but GSM8K never recovered, and the healed model still refused 25.78% of harmful prompts under an independent judge) ([Labonne 2024](https://huggingface.co/blog/mlabonne/abliteration); [Lee et al., CAST, ICLR 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.05907v3)).

Where abliteration *fails* is against models trained specifically to stop refusal from being a single concentrated feature. Three defense families measurably blunt single-direction abliteration on 2-9B models. **Extended-refusal fine-tuning** (topic overview → explicit refusal → ethical rationale) keeps post-abliteration refusal at **90-97% versus 14-21% for baselines**, because the explanatory text diffuses the refusal signal; but the defended models lose coherence and perplexity when abliterated, so they stay "safe" partly by breaking ([Abu Shairah et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.19056v2)). **Adversarial training against the refusal feature** — ReFAT and DeepRefusal — trains the model to still refuse when the direction is ablated, cutting refusal-ablation ASR from 53-93% to 10-21% (ReFAT) and to near **0% on Llama-3-8B and Gemma-7B** (DeepRefusal), though DeepRefusal still leaves 36.9% on Llama-2-7B ([Yu et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.20089v2); [Xie et al., EMNLP 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.15202v1)). **Circuit breakers** reroute harmful representations, dropping representation-space ASR to single digits ([Zou et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.04313v4)). Multi-direction ablation partly overcomes these — SOM still failed against a circuit-breaker model (Mistral-7B-RR, 25.79% max ASR) ([Piras et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08379v2)).

The decisive caveat is that **none of these defenses delivers tamper-resistance for open weights**. The durability literature shows "tamper-resistant" claims collapse under modest attack changes: TAR was broken by a schedule change or LoRA, RMU unlearning was *fully recovered* by directional ablation (WMDP-Bio 29.9 → 64.7), and circuit breakers hit 100% ASR under slightly stronger embedding attacks ([Qi et al. 2024, durability](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.07097v1); [Łucki et al., TMLR](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.18025v6); [Schwinn & Geisler 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.15902)). Critically, no published defense paper has been tested against *adaptive* abliteration — multi-direction, cone, SOM, or Heretic-optimized editing — so these defenses mostly change *which* cheap attack works (from directional ablation to LoRA or SFT), not whether the model can be opened. The only approach with order-of-magnitude fine-tuning resistance is **pretraining-data filtering** (Deep Ignorance), and it works by never learning the knowledge rather than by protecting refusal ([O'Brien, Casper et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06601)). A newer defensive paradigm, "Fool's Gold," concedes that refusal will be stripped and instead plants decoy answers that surface only in the abliterated state, so the attack "succeeds" on refusal metrics while yielding deliberately wrong content ([Russinovich 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17202)).

## Conclusion

The trajectory of abliteration research inverts a common intuition about open-weight safety. Refusal turned out to be *more* removable than expected — a shallow, formulaic, template-anchored feature that difference-in-means recovers and a rank-one weight edit erases for under $5 — yet the removal is *less* clean than the "single direction" headline implies, because refusal actually occupies a low-dimensional cone whose residual components, per-category directions and chain-of-thought reinforcement leave real work undone and real capability damage behind. The practical lesson for 2026 is that the best method is defined by its *constraints and its evaluation*, not by a clever direction: tune per-layer strength against a KL budget, protect the harmless manifold, prefer attention over MLP edits, escalate to multiple directions only when refusals plateau, and never trust a keyword-based refusal count over a quality-aware LLM judge on full-length held-out outputs.

The forward-looking implication is that abliteration should be understood as a *diagnostic of alignment depth* as much as an attack. Its reliability is an inverse measure of how deeply and diversely a model's refusals were trained: it works almost trivially on standard SFT/DPO models, degrades on reasoning models whose refusals live in the CoT, and fails on models whose refusals were deliberately spread — yet those same hardened models fall to a short fine-tune instead. No current open-weight defense survives an adaptive adversary with weight access; the honest frontier is not "stop abliteration" but either "never train the hazardous capability in" (data filtering) or "make the opened model untrustworthy" (decoys). For the practitioner, the sharpest unresolved risk is the quietest one: an abliterated model can pass every benchmark and still be a different decision-maker, so the real test is the deployment task, run under a byte-identical serving stack, not the leaderboard.
